# Lesson 04 — Compare the same operation

## Teaching contract

Teach one mechanism: compare the observed failure rates for the same operation, in the same observation window, using each version's own export-request denominator. The illustrative fixture is v1 control: 0 failed exports / 900 export requests = 0%; v2 candidate: 40 failed exports / 100 export requests = 40%. The candidate performed worse in this window; that comparison argues against promotion.

The numbers are authored illustration, not measured benchmark evidence. They neither prove the release caused the failures nor establish a universal decision threshold. Do not imply that a zero observed control rate guarantees future success. The scene contains only exports. Do not introduce pooled service counts, unrelated operations, a routing-share demonstration, statistical significance, a threshold line, rollback, or an automated release decision. “Control” means the existing version; “candidate” means the version being evaluated. Establish these roles with v1/v2 labels before using the shorter role names.

## Stage and visual grammar

Plan a finite 42-second, 1080 × 1920 scene at 30 fps and speed 1. Duration follows comprehension; remove slack rather than stretching to meet a target. Dark background, bright readable typography, restrained outlines, software query chips and metric panels. No hardware racks, physical metaphors, decorative traffic, narration paragraphs, or title-only opening. The opening title is two short lines: “Compare” / “export requests”. Keep “Illustrative counts” visible as a readable label throughout.

Three layouts serve three reasoning steps: a paired query-result view establishes scope; a full-width calculation view explains each denominator; vertically aligned metric rows compare the resulting rates. Keep v1 above v2 throughout. Their identity marks remain stable during transitions. Control uses a cool cyan marker and candidate a violet marker; red is reserved for failure quantities, never version identity. Written role labels and numerals carry meaning without reliance on colour.

At 350px playback width the scale factor is 350/1080. Essential labels use at least 52px source type, approximately 16.9px displayed. Main counts use 84px, approximately 27px displayed; rates use 116px, approximately 38px displayed. No informative microtext. Keep content between x=80 and x=1000. Break long labels into intentional lines, not compressed type. Use a 76px two-line opening title near y=230; scope chips occupy y=430–610; metric regions occupy y=760–1420; conclusions occupy y=1570–1740. Give each label at least 28px source clearance from outlines and animated highlights.

Actors are the export query, the shared observation-window selector, v1's result, v2's result, and a comparison summary. Query chips at (300,470) and (780,470) read “Exports” and “Window A”. They describe one stored illustrative observation window, with no invented duration. The result rows begin at (540,850) and (540,1230), each 920px wide. There are no request-flow wires: thin UI grouping rules connect the shared scope to both rows, with endpoints (300,540) → (300,640) → (120,640) → (120,1390), and (780,540) → (780,640) → (960,640) → (960,1390). These are static grouping marks, not network routes or traffic. Remove them before the enlarged arithmetic view.

## Sound and implementation detail

Use actor-specific synthesis throughout: query/window G5, v1 C5, v2 E5, comparison G4. Candidate failure cues drop its pitch to E4 while preserving its sonic identity. Centre the mix for the stacked layout; do not imply left/right actors that are absent visually. Send cues should be quieter than arrivals; the 40-failure cue is the strongest event but remains restrained. Use short sine-based tones, light harmonics, brief envelopes, and little echo. No music, voice, continuous clock, per-request barrage, or physical sound effects. Leave reading holds quiet. The lesson must remain comprehensible muted.

Schedule each transition and cue explicitly with scene time. Use labels, bars, boxes, `s.set`, `s.pulse`, and `s.sfx`; use a local drawing helper only if fraction alignment or scalable typography needs it. Derive the two rates and track fills from immutable fixture values so the numerals and geometry cannot diverge. All scene states are functions of time; seeking must not accumulate counts. Snapshot values appear together before individual explanations, preserving the common observation window. Avoid interpolating numeric strings through incorrect intermediate arithmetic.

## Planned verification and acceptance

This deliverable is a plan only; no rendering, listening, or playback verification is claimed. A later implementation should inspect encoded frames at each transition boundary, each calculation hold, and the busiest comparison frame, both full-size and at an actual 350px width. Check that version labels, operation, common window, both denominators, both rates, and illustrative status are readable without zoom. If they do not fit, remove decoration or split a reveal; never shrink essential text. Verify no percentage track appears to represent request volume.

Check arithmetic independently: 0/900 = 0 and 40/100 = 0.4; percentages are 0% and 40%. Check the candidate bar is 40% of its track, the control bar has zero filled width, and the two tracks have identical scales. Confirm neither denominator is replaced during reflow. Check each informative reveal has a corresponding cue and that audio peaks stay below -0.5 dB; actually listen before claiming audio quality. Decode the entire eventual encode and inspect duration and streaming metadata. The ending is deliberately finite and need not match the opening. No loop reset, traffic increase, or automatic promotion should appear.

A beginner should be able to answer after one viewing: what operation was compared; whether observations came from the same window; why the totals differ; which denominator belongs to which version; which observed rate was worse; and why this argues against promotion without proving cause. These are acceptance questions for later review, not claims of tested comprehension.
