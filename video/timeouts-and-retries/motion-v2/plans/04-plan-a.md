# 04 — Back off before retrying

## Teaching contract

Teach one mechanism: after three callers encounter a shared failure, waiting postpones the next attempts, and randomized variation in those waits can spread their arrivals. Use three independent read-only CI status calls, labelled A, B, and C, to one CI service. Presume these operations and observed errors are eligible for retry; this is an example policy, not a rule to retry every failure. Each caller makes exactly two total attempts: the initial request and one retry. All six receive an unavailable response. The service remains unavailable at the finite ending.

The central contrast is simultaneous arrival versus separated arrival, not failure versus guaranteed recovery. No overall deadline, remaining-time budget, cancellation lesson, exponential series, retry-layer discussion, or lesson 05 teaser. One retry cannot demonstrate exponential growth, so do not label this exponential backoff.

Minimum essential type is 48px at source, approximately 15.6px at a true 350px display width; title becomes about 26px. Essential strokes are at least 5px. No paragraph appears in the video.

Opening topology: caller panels centred at (210,650), (540,650), (870,650), each 240 × 180. Names A/B/C above two square attempt cells, with a shared “2 attempts max” chip at (540,500). CI service is a 440 × 220 panel centred at (540,1190), with three input anchors (400,1080), (540,1080), (680,1080). Wires run from caller bottoms (210/540/870,740) via corresponding x coordinates at y=930 to the three input anchors. All three packet animations use the same travel duration despite different path lengths. Requests are outlined chips; responses are small explicit red error chips moving back on those same routes. Attempt cells fill only on dispatch, never on receipt or during explanatory previews.

Middle layout: transform the caller panels into three horizontal scheduler rows centred at y=750,1030,1310. Row names sit at x=150, delay bars span x=270–790, and sampled value chips sit at x=865. A compact unavailable-service panel sits centred at (540,1600). Later, transform those same rows into an arrival trace, preserving identities and attempt cells. Layout transitions take 0.7–0.9 seconds and never move a request in flight. Remove old wires before changing the layout. This variation makes the scheduling relationship readable without keeping a tiny topology behind every beat.

## Event and count discipline

Maintain one event ledger in the eventual scene: A1/B1/C1 dispatch at 1.8; A2 at 20; B2 at 22; C2 at 24 playback seconds. Exactly six dispatches, six service receipts, six unavailable responses. Each caller increments once per dispatch and stops at two. Trace marks are a separate, non-dispatch visual type. The hypothetical immediate-arrival preview is hollow, explicitly conditional, and never updates the ledger. Do not replay a fully animated immediate-retry branch: that would confuse the requested two attempts per caller.

The sampled waits are a chosen illustrative realization, not measured timings, recommended defaults, or evidence that random choices cannot collide. There is no resampling until values differ. The semantic reference point for all waits is the shared failed-attempt completion; the policy explanation between 6 and 18 s freezes model time. At 18 s the model clock resumes from that reference point. Display a small “Paused” chip during policy inspection, replacing it with “Example delays” on resume. Avoid a global countdown: only local retry wait bars drain.

## Audio and implementation notes

Use scheduled pure-time state changes, packets, marks, and sound cues with the repository's pinned renderer if this plan is later implemented. Caller pitch identity must survive the switch from topology to scheduler rows. Pan from each visible actor's position, but keep all distinctions audible in mono. Layered simultaneous notes need lower gain to avoid clipping; target maximum peak below -1 dBFS. Service D3 belongs to the shared dependency; callers retain their distinct pitches on responses and stops. Waiting should sound quiet, not like an alarm. Do not equate an error with a machine crashing: no violent shake, crash blast, or reboot motif.
