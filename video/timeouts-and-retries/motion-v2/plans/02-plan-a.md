# Lesson 02 — A timeout is not a rollback

## Teaching target and concrete case

One mechanism: an attempt timeout ends the caller's wait; it does not establish what happened remotely. A release assistant asks a remote CI service for the status of build #42. The service is still assembling that status when the caller's waiting period ends. In this illustrative execution the lookup continues and finishes remotely. The assistant has received no usable response, so its build-status field remains unknown. The timeout is a known local event; the build's outcome is unknown to this caller. Never label the build failed merely because its status read timed out.

Use a single read, a single expiry, and one possible continuation. No second attempt, backoff, total-operation budget, subsequent polling, or preview of another lesson. The read does not start a build, cancel a build, or change a build's status. Remote work in the animation means processing the status lookup, not a newly launched CI pipeline. This distinction keeps the example concrete without conflating the request with the underlying build.

Proposed duration: 44 seconds, finite ending. The sequence item's existing 28-second duration is context, not a timing obligation. Playback seconds below describe editorial pacing, not a measured service latency or recommended timeout. An unnumbered attempt-wait meter avoids implying either.

## Sound design and pacing constraints

Caller identity is C; remote identity is E. Lower remote work ticks remain audibly separate from the caller clock. Use sparse event cues rather than an uninterrupted engine loop. Work ticks accompany visible progress only; their presence after caller ticks cease makes the separation audible. The timeout is the strongest local cue but sounds like a timer ending, never a service breaking. Limit short echoes to approximately 80 ms and do not let a caller tick echo suggest further waiting. Pan each cue gently to its actual visual anchor; pitch must distinguish actors even in mono. No narration or music is required. Keep predicted peak below -1 dBFS, then measure the eventual encode and listen before making any audio-quality claim.

Advance one focal event at a time. The terminal receipt transition is staged across several seconds, not a simultaneous flash of expired, unknown, closed socket, and remote completion. After expiry, quiet stillness at the caller is intentional; remote progress carries the next beat. Final four-second hold is deliberate reading time. The continuation qualifier must remain visible from its introduction through completion.

## Accuracy boundaries and evidence

Read sequence/lessons.json item 2 and the concept guide as the local scope sources. The exact scenario, coordinates, timings, lookup-progress marks, and sound choices above are illustrative design decisions, not source-backed measurements.

Primary sources checked 2026-11-03:

- [gRPC Deadlines](https://grpc.io/docs/guides/deadlines/), client/server deadline sections: client waiting ends at the deadline; gRPC can cancel the RPC automatically, but the application is responsible for stopping activity it spawned. This plan is transport-neutral and must not imply every timeout lacks cancellation signalling. Whether cancellation is propagated and honored depends on implementation. Cancellation of an RPC is not proof that all remote activity stopped.
- [AWS Builders’ Library: Timeouts, retries, and backoff with jitter](https://aws.amazon.com/builders-library/timeouts-retries-and-backoff-with-jitter/), introduction and timeout discussion: timeout limits caller waiting, and timeout/failure does not prove side effects did not occur. Use only that narrow principle here. The read-only CI illustration contains no write to reverse; retained work marks demonstrate absence of automatic rewind, not a transaction rollback experiment.

Cancellation means arranging for ongoing work to stop; rollback means undoing effects already produced. They are separate mechanisms whose behavior must be implemented and understood. Some implementations connect timeout to cancellation. The animation establishes only that timeout alone is insufficient evidence of cancellation or rollback. It makes no claim about which transport failure or scheduling delay prevented a timely response. The remote completion is not a late accepted result and is not evidence available to this caller.
