# Lesson 03 — Retry only when safe

## Teaching intention

One mechanism: the caller checks the documented operation contract before repeating a timed-out CI status read. It then asks the same question in a second bounded attempt and uses the response that actually arrives. Permission to repeat and evidence of success are different states, revealed at different moments.

Use a fictional, explicitly illustrative CI tool contract, not an invented claim about a named provider. Its documented operation is `Read status`, its target is `Build 42`, and its contract permits repetition after a response timeout. This example's read does not start, restart, or modify the build. A status read can usually be retried under its contract; the word “read” alone must never enable the retry. The second answer is a current observation, not necessarily the answer the first call would have returned.

Target duration: 39 seconds, finite ending. No voiceover or on-screen paragraphs. The existing lesson's 28-second metadata is context, not a timing constraint for this plan. No scene, media, metadata, or review artifact is produced by this planning pass.

## Stage and readable visual vocabulary

1080 × 1920 portrait, near-black background, warm white text, amber for unresolved waiting, cyan for requests, teal only for received evidence or verified eligibility. Reserve red for an actual rejection; a timeout does not turn the CI service red or make it crash.

Use a software inspector rather than a chain of generic boxes. A release assistant tool panel occupies x=90–990, y=490–1000. Inside it, a fixed operation header at y=570 identifies `Read status` and `Build 42`. Two attempt rows sit at y=720 and y=870. A single CI resource strip occupies x=170–910, y=1300–1510. The strip shows the same build identity and a status cell; no server racks, factories, shields, locks, physical keys, or bouncing objects. A result field in the assistant sits at y=960 and begins as `Status ?`.

A document tab labeled `Contract` expands over the work area during inspection; it is documentation associated with the tool, not another remote service or network hop. Its short rows appear sequentially: `Read status`, `Repeat: allowed`, `On: timeout`. An `Example contract` label prevents confusion with real provider documentation. Opening and checking this tab is a visualization of consulting known documentation; it does not imply fetching a fresh contract on every runtime call.

Title at y=270–390: `Retry only` / `when safe`, 92 px. Main labels 56–64 px, supporting labels at least 48 px, producing approximately 16 px or larger text at 350 px width. At most three short explanatory labels compete at once. Long terms wrap deliberately in the final inset. Maintain 64 px internal margins. Check shapes, attempt numbers, and literal status text as well as color.

Request rail runs from assistant port (350,1000) down to CI port (350,1300). Response rail runs from (730,1300) up to (730,1000). They appear only while active; labeled request chips carry `Build 42`, while the fixed operation header supplies `Read status`. The contract is never placed on either rail. During the contract and write caveat views, hide the rails completely.

## Sound and motion constraints

Caller identity is C5 (523 Hz), service identity G5 (784 Hz), documentation identity E5 (659 Hz). Sends use the sender's octave; responses land in the caller's register. Keep echoes short, around 70 ms, with sends below arrivals. Pan to the active panel or port, gently rather than hard left/right. All audio is synthesised; no speech, music bed, typing loop, or decorative ticking during editorial holds. The timeout cue is restrained because the service has not been shown to fail. Proposed peak ceiling: -1 dBFS. Future verification must distinguish actual listening from waveform inspection.

Transitions last roughly 0.4–0.7 s. Hold each learned state for about 2–2.5 s. Contract rows reveal one at a time, so the viewer follows the decision rather than reading a paragraph. Do not extend the runtime with repeated requests or idle motion. The five-second attempt bar runs from 17 s and stops at response acceptance at 21.2 s; this timing must remain coherent if animation timings change.

## Accuracy boundaries and evidence

- The local lesson 03 and concept JSON establish the CI read, second bounded attempt, conditional response use, and write caveat. Their broader material does not authorize adding backoff, jitter, total deadlines, exhaustion handling, polling, or later lessons to this cut.
- [RFC 9110 §9.2.2](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html#section-9.2.2), retrieved 2026-11-03: retry decisions depend on operation semantics; repeating an idempotent operation preserves its intended effect but need not yield an identical response. This supports the unchanged question and independently received answer. It does not document this fictional CI tool; the displayed tool contract is an explicit teaching assumption.
- [Stripe — Idempotent requests](https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests), retrieved 2026-11-03: service-side handling defines the meaning of the key, with version-specific behavior and constraints. This supports showing a key as insufficient without applicable service support. Do not import Stripe's payment behavior into the CI example or promise permanent deduplication.
- “Safe to repeat” does not mean “guaranteed to succeed.” An allowed timeout retry is not permission to retry every error. A missing response never proves no remote work occurred. A read name or HTTP-looking label alone is not the safety check.

All necessary text must be readable without zooming; remove secondary labels before reducing type. Verify attempt identifiers cannot be mistaken for build identifiers. Check that adding the idempotency-key header leaves the write retry inactive, and that the final read result never appears attached to the write example. Listen for consistent actor pitches and absence of a success sound during the safety check. A muted viewing should still reveal the operation match, enabled retry, unchanged build target, bounded wait, and evidence-dependent result. Ask a beginner what permitted attempt 2 and whether adding a key alone would permit a write retry; the visible contract checks should supply both answers.
