## Teaching and causal beats

- **A, 0–9.5 s:** Three agent → tool → result → agent circuits visibly drain a budget. This is the simplest account of a bounded loop, but the result turns green on every circuit, including unsuccessful ones. No changed data explains why it continues. At 3, 6 and 9 s a viewer can reasonably infer success already occurred. Neutral/amber continuation and a distinct success result would materially improve this.
- **A, 9.5–13.5 s:** Empty budget, red stop and cessation of packets communicate exhaustion. There is no visible enforcement actor, trace or human handoff. The restart/refill at about 13.5 s can look like the stopped agent simply grants itself more budget; separate the examples with a clear transition.
- **A, 16–24 s:** The second result routes to Complete with budget remaining. That contrast is useful, but the same generic Result and green treatment conceal the evidence that makes this result different. The roughly four-second final hold is longer than needed. The large empty title area weakens orientation. Main labels remain readable at 350px; Budget is small.
- **B, 1.5–6.5 s:** Running tests → one failed/exit 1 → result gate → patch 1 makes the reason for another edit concrete. The dedicated gate and one-directional packet sequence are stronger than A's opaque loop. Large type gives B the best phone legibility.
- **B, 6.5–10 s:** The gate retains FAIL → EDIT and the return wire stays amber while patch 1 is being tested and even briefly after pytest turns green. This stale permission state undermines the requirement for fresh evidence. Reset the gate to WAIT and dim the return wire as soon as the edit permit is consumed.
- **B, 10–14 s:** PASS → REVIEW then engineer review is the clearest success boundary of all three. It avoids implying that selected green tests authorize merge. The end state holds long enough without rushing.
- **B, 14.5–22 s:** A separately labelled task B, unchanged patch 0, zero collected/exit 5, a crossed return path, and evidence sent to the engineer demonstrate a genuinely different decision. The X is visible without reading the labels. This is the most immediately usable lesson for engineers delegating repairs. Need verify whether a mistyped keyword selector literally produces zero collected or zero selected.
- **B, 22–26 s:** The blocked outcome is clear; the final quiet hold could be shorter. Full-size and phone frames show no clipping or label collisions. Envelopes are anonymous, so the guide must explain what evidence is preserved.
- **C, 1–11 s:** Edit/test cycles accumulate 5 FAIL, 2 FAIL, PASS in a run log. The shrinking bars show change better than A's identical results. However counts alone cannot prove progress: tests could change, different failures can replace old failures, or a flaky test can pass. The selected suite and comparable evidence need to be explicit in the illustration or its boundaries.
- **C, 11–14 s:** DONE leaves the human inactive even though the guide says success permits review. Routing a review handoff on success would teach that boundary more honestly. At the exact 12 s frame the title is absent although it exists in neighbouring sheet samples; this appears to be an opacity/render-state fault, not an intentional end transition.
- **C, 16–26 s:** Three 3 FAIL entries trigger NO PROGRESS. Equal counts are insufficient evidence for that diagnosis. Preserve the useful repeated-observation mechanism, but show the same failure identity/evidence, or make the stop explicitly a budget/policy limit rather than a factual inference from counts. The warning mark after the second result and hard stop after the third are visually distinct, but no rule explains the third attempt threshold.
- **C, 26–29 s:** Dimming the loop and sending HAND OFF from the log to the human is a useful complementary mechanism. The log persists, so the viewer sees evidence retained across attempts. C's short moving chips and log text are appreciably smaller than B's at 350px. Several ripples cross log rows but do not obscure their full-size text. Full-size 22 and 27 s frames also lose the title (27 s loses the Human label), suggesting inherited canvas opacity.

## Preliminary comparison

B is the strongest prospective chapter entry: it teaches the difference between actionable test failure, a usable pass and absent evidence, with the clearest readable decision point. A is valuable as a complementary budget demonstration after its ambiguous result/reset states are repaired. C can add evidence-history and handoff teaching, but its equal-count inference and success boundary need correction before recommendation. These are teaching judgments, not a quota of passes or revisions.
